# Address shards

`lib/gobusgo/addresses.ex` and this directory build the street address index the Trip tab searches in the browser (GOB-361, decided by the measurements of GOB-354): the address points of King, Pierce, and Snohomish counties (GOB-363), normalized, grouped by street, cut into shards by the prefix of the street's name, and published through `Gobusgo.Writer` as files named by the SHA-256 of their content. One build reads all three counties and publishes one set of files. What a rider types never leaves the device: the page fetches the index and the one shard its street's name selects. No page reads the files yet.

## Files

- `addresses.ex` is the process that holds the manifest and the schedule of the build: it finds the newest stored manifest after a start, answers `manifest/1` and `built/1`, takes a build's manifest with `record/2`, and decides with `due?/1` whether a build is due.
- `normalize.ex` is the street normalizer, pure. `assets/js/address_normalize.js` holds the same rules for the browser; both read `test/fixtures/addresses/normalize.json` and must reach one result for each case, checked by `test/gobusgo/addresses/normalize_test.exs` and `node test/scripts/address_normalize_check.mjs`.
- `rows.ex` is the packed row of one address, pure. `accumulator.ex` collects a county's records into streets of packed rows and hashes the result, pure. `shards.ex` cuts the streets into shards, pure. `csv.ex` is the streaming CSV reader, pure.
- `source.ex` is the behavior contract of a county: how to fetch its whole layer into a file and how to read the file's rows as records. `source/king.ex`, `source/pierce.ex`, and `source/snohomish.ex` are the three counties'. `hub_export.ex` is the one-file export of the ArcGIS Hub that King reads, with the freshness check. `feature_pages.ex` is the lazy stream of a FeatureServer layer's pages, and `paged_layer.ex` writes a layer's pages to the CSV file the build reads, which Pierce and Snohomish share. `arc_gis.ex` reads the ArcGIS services an export comes through, and `http.ex` is the bounded request they share.
- `format.ex` is the encoder and decoder of the two kinds, shard and index, on `Gobusgo.Packed`, and implements `Gobusgo.Packed.Kind`. Pure. `assets/js/address_file.js` is the browser's decoder, checked by `node test/scripts/address_file_check.mjs`; `assets/js/address_lookup.js` is the lookup that reads its tables.
- `build.ex` is one run of the build.

## Source: King County

"Addresses in King County" is item `5aa0a3c77428491da8d73e9d069c10f6` of the county's open data portal, a FeatureServer layer at `https://services.arcgis.com/Ej0PsM5Aw677QF1W/arcgis/rest/services/ADDRESS_POINT_642/FeatureServer/0`. It holds one point per address point of the county, 675,399 on 2026-11-10, and the county maintains it weekly (its last edit was 2026-11-09). The terms are King County's, at <https://kingcounty.gov/en/dept/kcit/data-information-services/gis-center/about/terms-conditions-copyrights> — checked 2026-11-10: copying, distributing, and using the information are allowed, a sale of it needs a written agreement, and it is provided as is with no warranty. They state no attribution requirement; the application credits the county in the info panel anyway (`GobusgoWeb.AppInfo.address_sources/0`).

The integration reads these fields of the export: `HouseNum` (a number, optionally a space and one letter, as `"920 B"`), `PreDir`, `StreetName`, `SufType`, `SufDir`, `POSTALCTYNAME`, `LAT`, and `LON`. `LAT` and `LON` are WGS 84 degrees, so the server needs no projection library. A record is one distinct number, street, and postal city, at the mean of the positions of the points that share them.

## The download

Every download is bounded and runs under a timeout, and a county that fails to give a fresh file leaves the published files as they are. `Gobusgo.Addresses.Build` fetches the counties one after another, in the order of its `:sources` (King, Pierce, Snohomish), each into a directory of its own, and stops at the first that does not give a file: a build is the addresses of every county or nothing, so no build lacks a county the one before it held. A county's stale export (below) is `{:stale, details}` and a failed fetch `{:error, reason}`; the first is logged as `Address export stale` at `warning` with the `source`, the second as `Address shards not built` at `error` with `{:fetch, source, reason}`, and the manifest and the files stay. A county's file is deleted once the build has read it, and every file when the run ends, so the disk holds at most the files not yet read.

### One-file export: King

King's layer is fetched as the one-file export of the ArcGIS Hub, not in pages of 100 items, since the export is one file with a size bound and a deadline on its request (decided in GOB-354). `hub.arcgis.com` and the blob store publish no IPv6 address, and production reaches only hosts that do, so this path fails there; GOB-380 moves King to paging its FeatureServer. `Gobusgo.Addresses.HubExport.fetch/2`, which `Source.King.fetch/2` calls, does it for a layer:

1. reads the layer's last edit, `editingInfo.lastEditDate` in milliseconds, from `<layer>?f=json`, and its record count from `<layer>/query?where=1=1&returnCountOnly=true&f=json` (<https://developers.arcgis.com/rest/services-reference/enterprise/layer-feature-service/> and <https://developers.arcgis.com/rest/services-reference/enterprise/query-feature-service-layer/> — checked 2026-11-10);
2. asks the Hub for the export with `GET https://hub.arcgis.com/api/download/v1/items/<item>/csv?layers=0&redirect=false` and asks again every 10 seconds, at most 30 times, until the answer, a `200` JSON object (or a `202` one, below), has `status` `"Completed"` and a `resultUrl`. Esri has not published this API (a search of its documentation on 2026-11-10 found no reference for it), so the contract is what it answered on 2026-11-10 and 2026-11-11: a `202` with `status` `"Pending"` and the message `Up to date download file is being generated. Please check back again later.` while it makes a fresh file, about one answer in five on 2026-11-11, which is asked for again like any other status that is not ready (before 2026-11-11 it was read as an unexpected status and failed the build 9 s after a start); `400` `Downloads are not supported for this item.` for an item that cannot be downloaded, `404` `Layer does not exist.` for a layer the item lacks, and a `status` of `"Failed"` for a failed export. A status other than `"Completed"` and `"Failed"` is read as not ready yet;
3. follows `resultUrl`, which answers `302` to a signed address of the blob store under `*.az.arcgis.com`, at most three redirects and only to an `https` address under `arcgis.com`, and never logs an address, since the signature is a credential;
4. checks the headers of the blob before it writes any of the body: `x-ms-meta-lasteditdate` and `x-ms-meta-recordcount` are the layer's last edit and record count when the export was made (<https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/rest/api/storageservices/get-blob-properties> — checked 2026-11-10). An export whose last edit is older than the layer's, or whose count is not the layer's, is stale, and so is one with no such headers;
5. streams the body to a file in a directory of the build's own, at most 512 MiB of it (King's export was 243 MB on 2026-11-10, 242,668,090 bytes), with a deadline of 900 seconds on the request and no retry, since the scheduler's backoff repeats a failed build.

A stale export is a known outcome, returned as `{:stale, details}` and logged as `Address export stale` at `warning`; the build keeps the current files. A file read to its end that holds a different count of records from its layer's fails the build.

### Paged layers: Pierce and Snohomish

Snohomish County's Hub export answered from a stale cache (397,283 records against the layer's 397,927 on 2026-11-10), so `Gobusgo.Addresses.Source.Snohomish.fetch/2` pages the FeatureServer instead (decided in GOB-354). `Gobusgo.Addresses.Source.Pierce.fetch/2` pages its FeatureServer too, because production reaches only hosts that publish an IPv6 address, plus S3, and the Hub's export host does not: on 2026-11-11 Google's DNS answered AAAA records for `services2.arcgis.com` (Pierce) and `services6.arcgis.com` (Snohomish) and none for `hub.arcgis.com` or `www.arcgis.com`. A county added here reads only from a host with an AAAA record. `Gobusgo.Addresses.FeaturePages.stream/2` is the lazy stream of its pages, which emits `{:page, features}` for each page and one `{:error, reason}` at a failure:

1. each request is `GET <layer>/query?where=OBJECTID>last&outFields=OBJECTID,…&outSR=4326&orderByFields=OBJECTID&resultRecordCount=100&returnGeometry=true&f=json`, at most 100 features, the style guide's ceiling for one request though the layer allows 2,000, in the order of `OBJECTID` and above the last `OBJECTID` of the page before, so no request reads a deep offset (<https://developers.arcgis.com/rest/services-reference/enterprise/query-feature-service-layer/> — checked 2026-11-10). A page of fewer than 100 features ends the stream, and so does an empty page;
2. each request has the 60-second deadline of `Gobusgo.Addresses.Http` and at most 4 MiB of body, and the stream is never read past 5,000 pages, against the 3,980 Snohomish's layer needs and the 4,167 Pierce's does; a layer that needs more is an error, `:page_count_exceeded`, and a full page whose `OBJECTID`s do not rise is `:no_progress`;
3. a request is retried for a failure the service marks transient only: a network fault, a deadline, a status of 408, 429, or 5xx, or an error object whose `code` is one of those, which ArcGIS answers with a `200`. It makes at most 3 attempts, waits from a base of 500 ms doubling with jitter, and takes a `Retry-After` in seconds in place of the computed delay, at most 30 seconds. Any other status or error code is a value, `{:unexpected_status, status}` or `{:service_error, code}`, and a page that is not a feature list is `{:malformed, path}`.

`Gobusgo.Addresses.PagedLayer.fetch/2`, which both counties call, reads the layer's last edit and count, writes the features of each page to a CSV file of the county's fields, with `x` and `y` for the position, at most 512 MiB of it, so the build reads it with `Gobusgo.Addresses.Csv` like King's export, and returns the count it wrote. The file is CSV because the build's process sweeps its whole heap at every collection: measured on 2026-11-10, Snohomish's rows read as one JSON object a line took 454 seconds over a heap holding the other counties' streets, against 15 for Pierce's CSV. A layer that holds another count of features than it said when the read began, because the county edited it meanwhile, is an error, `{:record_count_differs, read, layer}`, and the scheduler's backoff reads it again. Paging reads the layer itself, so its result is never stale.

Measured on 2026-11-10 from a development machine: paging the layer's 397,927 features took 3,982 requests (3,980 pages, the layer's description, and its count) and 704 seconds, 0.18 s a request, and wrote a 89,769,999-byte file. The run deadline below is set from that. A build that must spend less on paging skips a county's download while its `lastEditDate` is the one the current build recorded in the manifest's `sources`; this build does not, since 12 minutes a day fits the deadline.

The export files are deleted as described above.

## Normalization

`Gobusgo.Addresses.Normalize` and `assets/js/address_normalize.js` normalize a street so the name a county publishes and the name a rider types reach one key. A county's fields are joined into one line and parsed as typed text, so a county that writes "WEST" in the name and a rider who types "W" reach one street:

- text is upper case with punctuation dropped; `&`, `/`, `@`, `#`, and `,` are tokens of their own; at most 24 tokens are read;
- a suffix directional and a type are read from the end and a prefix directional from the start, each only while a name token remains; directionals and types become their USPS Publication 28 abbreviations (<https://pe.usps.com/text/pub28/28apc_002.htm> — checked 2026-11-10); "NORTH EAST" merges to NE only where two tokens follow it;
- Pierce County's compound types, ST CT, AVE CT, ST PL, AVE PL, ST LP, LN CIR, LN RD, and LN CT, stay one type, and its "LP" is LOOP; the 7,207 distinct street spellings of Pierce County's active points and the 12,488 of Snohomish County's were run through the normalizer on 2026-11-10, and none reached a wrong type, directional, or name, so the shared fixture needed no new case (GOB-363);
- a numbered name is its digits and ordinal suffix: "12", "12th", and "twelfth" are 12TH; a number of more than nine digits is left as it is;
- a unit is dropped from a typed query: APT, UNIT, STE, SUITE, BLDG, FL, RM, SPC, LOT, #, and NO, with the token after each.

A bare number is an ordinal wherever it stands in a name, so "SR 520" reads as "SR 520TH" on both sides and still reaches one key.

## Records, streets, and memory

`Gobusgo.Addresses.Accumulator` keeps a street's addresses as a binary of fixed 13-byte rows (`Gobusgo.Addresses.Rows`): the house number as `u32`, a letter suffix as one byte, and the position as two `i32` microdegrees. A county's rows are gathered per street, in a tail of at most 4 rows that is packed into a chunk when it fills, so the addresses of the three counties are a few binaries per street and no term per address. The counties are read one after another into the one accumulator, so a street of one name in one postal city that two counties share is one street. A record is counted and left out when its house number is not a whole number with at most one letter (a "1/2" is `suffix_unsupported`), when its street name is empty, when its position is not on the globe, when its position is outside the bounds, or when its source marks it inactive (Pierce's `TEMPORARY` points, counted as `inactive`). The bounds are the union of the bounds of the archives of the basemap's manifest, `<GOBUSGO_DATA_DIR>/basemap/manifest.json`. With no manifest the build keeps every position and says so in no log; a development directory has none, and production has one.

The build runs in a process of its own with `fullsweep_after: 0` and a heap cap of `:heap_words_max` that counts the binaries the process references (`Gobusgo.Addresses.Build.heap_cap/1`), killed and logged as `Address shard build killed` at `error` when it outgrows it. While it waits, the run samples that process's heap and logs the peak as `heap_words_peak`.

## Shards and files

`Gobusgo.Addresses.Shards` groups the streets by the normalized street name with its spaces removed, and splits a group by the next letter until the group's encoded content is at most 65,536 bytes before compression, which gives King County's largest shard 41,508 bytes gzipped, under the 48 KB of GOB-354's measurement, where 131,072 gave 82,121, because the transfer of the shard is what a cold query waits for (GOB-362) (`Gobusgo.Addresses.Build.shard_bytes_max/0`). The bound is on the encoded size, measured by encoding each candidate group with `Gobusgo.Addresses.Format.encode_shard/1`, except a group whose `Gobusgo.Addresses.Format.shard_bytes_min/1`, 4 bytes an address, is already over the bound, which is split without being encoded. A name that equals a prefix that was split further stays in a shard named by the prefix and `$`. A group whose names all continue with one letter descends without a shard of its own, and a group at 32 letters, or one name over the bound, is a shard as large as it is.

A file is a packed container (`lib/gobusgo/packed/AGENTS.md`) named `<prefix>-<SHA-256 of its gzip stream>.bin.gz` by `Gobusgo.Packed.destination/1`, `addresses` for a shard and `addresses-index` for the index, and stored through the writer at `addresses/dt=<build date>/<name>` with `reader_keep_seconds` of a year, so a reader may keep it for a year. The SHA-256 is the entry's `sha256` and the stream's bytes are its `byteSize`. The index's entries hold each shard's prefix, size, and hash and no file name or directory, so the same shards give the same index and the same names on every day, and a shard is stored beside the index.

## The kinds

Both files are packed containers (`lib/gobusgo/packed/AGENTS.md`, GOB-369), version 1, written and read by `Gobusgo.Addresses.Format`. Kind 3, prefix `addresses`, is a shard; kind 4, prefix `addresses-index`, is the index. The header's record count is the count of streets in a shard and of shards in the index.

A shard holds one record per street, a street being the addresses of one normalized street in one postal city, in the order of the street's name key, and 8 sections after the string table:

1. names, a `u32` column of the string index of each street's normalized name;
2. prefix directionals, a `u32` column of string indexes, the empty string for none;
3. types, a `u32` column of string indexes;
4. suffix directionals, a `u32` column of string indexes;
5. postal cities, a `u32` column of string indexes;
6. numbers: per street a varint count of addresses, at least one, then per address the varint difference of its house number from the one before it, the first from 0, so numbers do not decrease within a street (a repeated number with another letter has difference 0);
7. suffixes, a `u8` column of one byte per address in street order, 0 for none or the ASCII code of an upper case letter;
8. positions: per street a path of the container, one point per address, latitude and longitude in microdegrees, each point a zigzag varint difference from the one before it, the first from (0, 0).

The string table interns each street's name, prefix directional, type, suffix directional, and city in that order, by first use, so a type or city used by many streets is stored once. The index holds one record per shard, in the order of their prefixes, and 3 sections: prefixes, a `u32` column of string indexes, ending in `$` for the shard of the streets named by the prefix alone; byte sizes, a `u32` column of the sizes of the shards' gzip streams; and digests, 32 bytes a shard, the SHA-256 of that stream. The index holds no file names: a shard's file is in the index's directory, and `Gobusgo.Packed.file_name/1` and `packedFileName` in `assets/js/packed_container.js` name it from the kind and the digest, `addresses-<digest>.bin.gz`, so the digest is the one owner of the name.

String columns are `u32`, where a `u16` would fit the shards the build keeps, because `Gobusgo.Addresses.Shards` measures groups of streets before it splits them, and a group of a county with more streets than King's holds more than 65,535 strings. A shard holds no prefix: the index names it.

The columns and the container's varints are laid out so a browser reads a shard in one pass over typed arrays, with no object per address. `assets/js/address_file.js` decodes a shard into `{streetCount, strings, names, preDirections, types, sufDirections, cities, addressStarts, numbers, suffixes, positions}`: `strings` is every string of the file, decoded eagerly; the five street columns are `Uint32Array`s of indexes into it; street `s` holds addresses `addressStarts[s]` up to `addressStarts[s + 1]`, a `Uint32Array` of `streetCount + 1` starts; `numbers` is a `Uint32Array` of house numbers, `suffixes` a `Uint8Array`, and `positions` an `Int32Array` of latitude and longitude in turn. It decodes an index into `{shardCount, prefixes, paths, byteSizes, sha256s}`: arrays of strings, a `Uint32Array`, an array of lower case hexadecimal digests, and `paths`, the file names `packedFileName` makes from them. `loadAddressShard` and `loadAddressIndex` expand a fetched gzip stream first. Each returns `{kind: "decoded", table}` or `{kind: "refused", reason}`, never a throw. `assets/js/address_lookup.js` reads these tables (the next section); no page calls it yet.

A reader refuses another version, the other kind, another section count, a string index past the table, a column of another length, numbers whose counts run past their section or that leave bytes after the last street, or whose sum passes 32 bits, a suffix that is not 0 or a letter, a path of another length than its street's addresses, a position outside the globe, and a digest section of another length, each as a value (`AddressFileRefusal` and the container's own reasons; the Elixir names are `:bad_string_index`, `:bad_numbers`, `:bad_suffix`, and `:count_mismatch` beside the container's). A shard expands to at most `Gobusgo.Addresses.Format.expanded_bytes_max(:shard)`, 1 MiB, and an index to at most `expanded_bytes_max(:index)`, 1 MiB; `ADDRESS_SHARD_EXPANDED_BYTES_MAX` and `ADDRESS_INDEX_EXPANDED_BYTES_MAX` repeat them in JavaScript, and a test reads the JavaScript constants and compares them. A shard the build writes is within the bound by `Format.assert_expanded_size/2`; one over it, which only a street name whose streets encode to more than 1 MiB gives, stops the build.

`mix gobusgo.packed.decode <file> --kind-module Gobusgo.Addresses.Format` prints a shard or an index as JSON.

### Fixtures

## Lookup

`assets/js/address_lookup.js` is pure and takes decoded tables: the caller fetches the files, expands them with `loadAddressIndex` and `loadAddressShard`, and keeps the decoded shards for the session. What a rider types never leaves the device, since the page asks for the index and the one shard a street's name selects (GOB-362).

- `shardFor({index, text})` parses `text` with `address_normalize.js` and returns the shard of the street's name: the shard of the name alone, `<name key>$`, where the index names one, otherwise the shard of the longest prefix of the key. Its outcome is `found` with the shard's `path`, `byteSize`, `sha256`, and `prefix`, `noShard`, or `notAnAddress` for text with no house number and street.
- `searchAddress({text, table, near})` returns `found` with at most five `results`, `noStreet` where no street of the shard agrees, `pastEnd` where a street agrees and none has a same-side neighbor on both sides of the number, or `notAnAddress`. `near` is a `[lon, lat]` position, the map's center.
- A street agrees when its normalized name equals the typed one, a typed type or directional equals the street's (one left out matches any), and a typed locality starts the postal city. A ZIP code is read and left out, since a shard holds none, and a unit is dropped.
- A number the shard holds is answered at its point: the address with the typed letter, or else the first address with that number. A number it lacks is placed by linear interpolation between the nearest addresses of its parity on either side of it (the same side of the street), which GOB-354 measured at p50 11 m and p90 43 m on held-out King addresses.
- Results rank held points before interpolated ones, then the fewer parts (prefix directional, type, suffix directional) filled in, then the distance from `near`. Each carries its `label`, `city`, `lat`, `lon`, `placement`, `filledInCount`, and `distanceM`.

`node test/scripts/address_lookup_check.mjs` checks each rule over the shared fixtures.

## Manifest

`GET /api/addresses/manifest` answers the manifest from `Gobusgo.Addresses` (`lib/gobusgo_web/AGENTS.md`). It holds `builtAt`, `sourceLastEditAt` (the newest last edit of the counties), `sources` (one entry per county with its `name`, `lastEditAt`, and `recordCount`), `inputsSha256`, `addressCount`, `streetCount`, `index` (`path`, `byteSize`, `sha256`), and `shards`, each with the same three fields and its `prefix`. The manifest is stored last, after every file it names, at `addresses/dt=<build date>/manifest-<build time>-<inputsSha256>.json`, so a start finds the newest build by its newest manifest and a build that fails half way leaves no manifest naming a missing file.

## Schedule and refresh

`Gobusgo.Addresses.spec/1` is the scheduler that checks every hour whether a build is due. A build is due when there is none, at any time, or when the current build is from an earlier UTC date and the hour has reached 10, so the build runs once a day at a fixed slot, and a start that finds a stored build downloads and builds nothing. A start with no stored build builds at once.

A build with three sources decides in this order: it fetches every county, and the first that is stale or fails ends the run with the files as they are (the next hourly check tries again, the scheduler backing a failed run off); once every county is fetched it reads them and fails the run if any county's records read differ in count from its layer's; and only then does it compare the input to the current build's, below. The input is every county's accepted addresses together, so a change to any one county changes the hash and the files, and a build whose three counties are the same as the current build's changes nothing. The run's deadline is 3,300,000 ms, 55 minutes, which the scheduler requires to be under the hour between checks: the measured run is about 16 minutes, Snohomish's paging 12 of them, and the rest is room for two Hub exports that are slow to make (each polled for up to 5 minutes) and for a layer that answers slowly.

A build whose input hashes to the current build's, and whose current build is from today, builds nothing and logs `Address shards unchanged`. The hash is of the streets after the bounds are applied, the normalized key, the city, and the rows, so a change to any of them, or to the bounds, changes it. Otherwise it writes every file again under the build date, which keeps every file the manifest names inside the seven days the bucket and `Gobusgo.RetentionPolicy` keep (`GOB-365` holds the lifecycle rule and the CloudFront behavior). The application starts the build wherever the sources are scheduled.

## Measurements

A real King County build on 2026-11-10 with no basemap bounds, so every position stayed: 675,399 records read, 674,833 accepted, 566 left out as `suffix_unsupported`, 653,863 distinct addresses on 17,012 streets, in 333 shards. The shards' gzip streams total 2,766,230 bytes, at a median of 5,682, a 90th percentile of 17,874, and a maximum of 41,508 bytes; before compression they total 4,220,068 bytes, at a median of 8,576, a 90th percentile of 27,558, and a maximum of 65,477 against the bound of 65,536. The index is 13,264 bytes compressed. A bound of 131,072 gave 104 shards, a gzip maximum of 82,121, and an index of 9,191 bytes with the file names in it, which at the bound of 65,536 made it 28,243; a bound of 57,344 gave 376 shards and a gzip maximum of 37,616. The build took about 55 s: 11 s to fetch the export, 41 s to read it, and 2 s to cut and write.

Memory, measured as the build's process memory, `mix run`'s maximum resident size under `/usr/bin/time -l`, and the release image's anonymous memory started as `mix gobusgo.start_memory` starts it but with the network on so the export downloads (one-off, not part of CI, which has no network). With a tail of 256 rows per street the build process peaked at 82 MiB, `mix run` at 514 MiB, and the image at 734 MiB at 47.6 s. At 4 rows, which keeps a street's recent rows off the heap in small chunks, they are 33 MiB, 373 MiB, and 588 MiB at 67.1 s, with the routing and tile builds done at 8.2 s and 536 MiB. The referenced binaries are 15 MiB or less at any sample, so the heap, not off-heap data, was the growth. `Format.shard_bytes_min/1` lets the split pass over the encoding of the groups that are certainly too large; without it the build peaked at 15.4 million heap words, 364 MiB of BEAM memory, and 886 MiB resident.

`mix gobusgo.start_memory --image gobusgo:local` passed at a peak of 522 MiB against the fixture, with the address process and its scheduler in the application; the container has no network, so the build's download fails there and that run does not cover a real build's memory.

## Logs

One line per run, at the decision point: `Address shards built` and `Address shards unchanged` at `info`, `Address export stale` at `warning`, and `Address shards not built` and `Address shard build killed` at `error`. Each carries counts and times as properties (records read, accepted, and left out by cause, streets, addresses, shards, bytes, and the time of each step, and for each county its records read and accepted, its bytes, and its fetch and read times as `<county>_read_count` and the like) and never an address of the download. `Address manifest discovery failed` is logged at `warning` by the process when a start cannot read a stored manifest.

## Adding a county

A county is a module that implements `Gobusgo.Addresses.Source`: its `fetch/2`, which writes the layer to a file and says when it was last edited and how many records it has, and its `records/1`, which reads the file as records. Normalization, grouping, sharding, and writing are shared. A county pages its FeatureServer with `Gobusgo.Addresses.PagedLayer.fetch/2`, as Pierce and Snohomish do, from a host that publishes an IPv6 address; the Hub export of `Gobusgo.Addresses.HubExport.fetch/2`, which King reads, cannot run in production. The module joins the `:sources` of `Gobusgo.Addresses.Build`, with a section here for its item, service address, fields, refresh, and terms with the date each was checked, and a credit with its disclaimers in `GobusgoWeb.AppInfo.address_sources/0`.
