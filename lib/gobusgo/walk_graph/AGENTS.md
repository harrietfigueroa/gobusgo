# Walk graph

The walk graph is the pieces of street, sidewalk, path, crossing, and steps a Trip tab journey's walks are drawn along, built by hand from a Geofabrik Washington extract of OpenStreetMap and published as packed containers of kinds 8 and 9, sharded by tile (GOB-467). Nothing in the application builds them or reads them on a start; the Trip tab reads the shards a walk needs in the browser and draws the walk along the shortest path between its ends, so the rider's position never leaves the device (GOB-241). Only the drawing follows the graph: a walk's planned time stays the straight-line time of the router (`lib/gobusgo/routing/AGENTS.md`), and GOB-468 will plan over the same files. This file holds the walkable rule, the format, the tiles, the publication, the browser's reading, and the measurements.

## Files

- `classification.ex` decides which ways are walkable and their class, and holds the `osmium tags-filter` expression. Pure.
- `edges.ex` cuts a way into edges at its junctions. `simplify.ex` drops the positions of an edge within a tolerance of the line its neighbors make. `tiles.ex` holds the tile grid and cuts an edge into the pieces of its tiles. Pure.
- `selection.ex` reads the export three times, the nodes, the ways' references, and the ways again, and returns the pieces by tile with the counts of what became of each way. It holds two ETS tables for the length of the call.
- `extractor.ex` runs `osmium` through `Gobusgo.Command`; `plan/1` is the step arguments, pure. The lines of the export are read with `Gobusgo.Intersections.Extractor.lines/1`, and the OPL reader is `Gobusgo.Intersections.Opl`, the intersections' own.
- `build.ex` runs the extractor and the selection. `select/1`, the part after osmium, is deterministic.
- `format.ex` is the encoder and decoder of the two kinds, shard and index, on `Gobusgo.Packed`, and implements `Gobusgo.Packed.Kind`. Pure.
- `publication.ex` orders and encodes each tile's shard, stores the files and the manifest through the writer.
- `graph.ex` builds the graph over the pieces of some decoded shards and snaps a point onto it; `search.ex` selects the tiles a walk reads and finds the shortest walk along it, one to one and one to many. Both are pure and read no file; `lib/gobusgo/routing/walk_links.ex` and `lib/gobusgo/routing/walk_links/store.ex` read the shards, hand them to the search, and measure a snapshot's walking links with them (GOB-468).
- `lib/mix/tasks/gobusgo.walk_graph.build.ex` is the Mix task. `lib/gobusgo_web/controllers/walk_graph_manifest_controller.ex` serves the manifest.
- `assets/js/core/walk_graph_file.js` decodes the kinds and checks the manifest, `assets/js/core/walk_path.js` finds the path, `assets/js/walk_graph_fetch.js` fetches the files, and `assets/js/walk_path_reader.js` holds the tiles and the lines a page draws; `journeyWalkEnds`, `walkEndsOf`, and `walkKey` of `assets/js/core/trip_plan.js` name each walk's ends and `journeyFeatures` draws the line. `node test/scripts/walk_path_check.mjs` checks the core.

## Source and license

The source is OpenStreetMap through Geofabrik's Washington extract, the one the places file and the intersections read (`lib/gobusgo/intersections/AGENTS.md`, "Source and license"): the date of its replication timestamp is the build's `sourceDate`. The graph is a derivative database under the ODbL, <https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/1-0/> — checked 2026-11-10: the manifest names the license and the credit "© OpenStreetMap contributors", the info panel's credit for the intersection files covers it (`GobusgoWeb.AppInfo.intersection_source/0`), and the files are served for anyone to fetch from the same directory.

## Walkable ways (decided in GOB-467)

`Gobusgo.WalkGraph.Classification.class/1` takes a way's tags. A way is walkable when its `highway` is `footway`, `path`, `pedestrian`, `track`, `platform`, `steps`, `living_street`, `residential`, `service`, `unclassified`, `tertiary`, `secondary`, `primary`, the three links, or one of `cycleway`, `bridleway`, `trunk`, `trunk_link` under a condition, and no tag forbids foot traffic.

- `motorway` and `motorway_link` are never walkable, whatever `foot` says.
- `trunk` and `trunk_link` are walkable where `foot` is `yes`, `designated`, or `permissive`, or `sidewalk` is `both`, `left`, `right`, or `yes`, or `sidewalk:both`, `sidewalk:left`, or `sidewalk:right` is `yes` (`sidewalk=separate` does not count, since the sidewalk is a way of its own then): a trunk road in Seattle, Aurora Avenue among them, is one people cross and walk beside, and elsewhere one they must not walk on.
- `cycleway` and `bridleway` are walkable where `foot` allows it, as above; a shared-use trail is tagged so.
- `foot=no` and `foot=private` exclude a way. `access=no` and `access=private` exclude a way unless `foot` allows it. This leaves out private driveways and gated service roads, 103,989 of the 895,431 ways of the build below.
- `area=yes` excludes a way, since a plaza's outline is not a line to walk along. `construction`, `proposed`, and every other `highway` value are excluded. `corridor` and `elevator` are walkable paths, the ways of a station's concourse.
- A `service` road is walkable unless private: an alley or a parking aisle is a way through a block on foot, which the intersections do not want and a walk does.
- A sidewalk is a way of its own where OpenStreetMap maps one (`footway=sidewalk`), and the `sidewalk` tag of a road adds no line. A road that has no mapped sidewalk is walked on, since a pedestrian does walk it.

The class a piece carries is `path` (0), `sidewalk` (1), `crossing` (2: `footway=crossing`, `cycleway=crossing`, or a `crossing` tag), `steps` (3), or `street` (4). The drawing ignores it; it is in the format so a planner can weigh a street against a sidewalk and avoid steps (GOB-468) without a new version.

## Graph and pieces

A junction is a node that two ways list, or one way lists twice, or that ends a way; a way's interior node with one reference is no junction. An edge runs between two junctions along a way, with the positions between them, simplified (below). An edge is cut where it leaves a tile (`Gobusgo.WalkGraph.Tiles`): the piece in the tile ends at the first position of the next tile, which the next piece starts from, so the two share that position and the browser joins pieces by position, whatever shard each is in. No position is invented. A piece belongs to the tile of its first position, and an edge of more than 1,000 positions is cut at a vertex into pieces that share it, so a piece's count fits 16 bits. A closed edge, whose first and last position are one (7,369 of 1.7M pieces in the first build: turning circles and closed footways), is cut at its middle position into two, and one that has no length is dropped, so the graph has no loop edge: a search costs a loop's two halves and the drawing slices the same halves. Positions are `round(degrees * 100,000)`, the graph's unit, 1.1 m of latitude and 0.75 m of longitude at 47.6 degrees north: two nodes that round to one position are one node, and a position is identical in two shards because it is rounded once.

An edge is simplified by the Douglas-Peucker rule to 1 unit, about 1 m, in a plane where longitude is scaled by the cosine of the edge's first latitude. The ends stay, so the topology does not change. The graph has no direction: a piece is walked either way.

## The kinds

Both files are packed containers (`lib/gobusgo/packed/AGENTS.md`), version 1, written and read by `Gobusgo.WalkGraph.Format`. Kind 8, prefix `walk-graph`, is a shard; kind 9, prefix `walk-graph-index`, is the index.

A shard holds the pieces of one tile, one record per piece, with an empty string table and 3 sections: classes, a `u8` column; counts, a `u16` column of the positions of each piece, at least two; and positions, one path of the container through every position of every piece in record order, in the graph's units, so a difference from the position before is small. Pieces are ordered by the Morton code of their first position's offset inside the tile, which keeps the jump from one piece's last position to the next piece's first short.

The index holds the build identifier as the one entry of its string table, and one record per run of tiles that neighbor in a row, in the order of rows and then columns, with 3 sections: rows, an `i32` column; starts, an `i32` column of the run's first column; lengths, a `u16` column, at least one. A shard's file is `walk-graph-<build identifier>-<row>_<column>.bin.gz` in the index's directory (`Gobusgo.WalkGraph.Format.shard_file_name/1`, `walkGraphShardName` in `assets/js/core/walk_graph_file.js`). The build identifier is the first 16 hexadecimal digits of the SHA-256 over every shard's tile and stream digest, so a name fixes its file's content, a file is cacheable for a year as immutable, and the index needs no digest per shard: with 14,818 shards, a digest of 32 bytes a shard would have made the index 480 KB where it is 3,318 bytes. A shard and an index each expand to at most 1 MiB.

`assets/js/core/walk_graph_file.js` decodes a shard into `{pieceCount, classes, pieceStarts, points}` as typed arrays and an index into `{buildId, tileKeys}`, a Set of `<row>_<column>`. A reader refuses another version, the other kind, another section count, a class past 4, a count under 2, counts the path does not hold, bytes after the path, a build identifier that is not 16 lower case hexadecimal digits, a piece that starts and ends at one position, a run of no tiles, and an index of more than 200,000 tiles, each as `{kind: "refused", reason}`, never a throw. `acceptWalkManifest` refuses a manifest whose tile size, units, or `kindVersion` differ from the reader's, so a page refuses a newer build before it fetches a shard.

`mix gobusgo.packed.decode <file> --kind-module Gobusgo.WalkGraph.Format` prints a shard or an index as JSON.

## Tiles and shard size (measured in GOB-467)

A tile is 1,000 units of latitude by 1,500 of longitude, 0.01 by 0.015 degrees, 1.11 by 1.13 km at 47.6 degrees north, numbered by the floor of a position over the size, so a tile in Washington has a positive row and a negative column. On 2026-11-14 from Geofabrik's extract of 2026-11-08T21:21:00Z, cut to the basemap manifest's bounds (46.888124 to 48.642846 north, -123.082727 to -121.533451 east), the graph is 5,174,648 nodes, 1,276,111 junctions, 784,415 walkable ways of 895,744, 1,713,422 pieces, and 5,044,891 positions after simplification (6.58 million without it, in the first build), in **14,818 shards**, 11,416,109 bytes gzip in all. A shard is at a median of 328 bytes, a 90th percentile of 1,870, and a maximum of 19,482 gzip; the index is 3,318 bytes.

The tile size and the encoding were chosen by building the same graph at three tile sizes and with and without the simplification and the unit of the 100,000th of a degree. Microdegree positions with no simplification took 19,978,643 bytes at this tile size, 20 MB; the units cut it to 11.4 MB with the simplification. A walk of 100 to 400 m at a random bearing in a 3 km square around four centers read the tiles its box widened by 150 m touches, as the reader of the first build chose them; the table and the sentence after it measure that reader, and a walk now reads 9 to 14 tiles:

| Tile (lat by lon units) | Shards | Gzip bytes of a walk downtown Seattle (median, p90, max) | Files |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 500 by 750 | 3.0 to 3.3 on average, 6 at most | 9,211, 17,863, 26,527 | 49,316 |
| **1,000 by 1,500** | **1.8 to 2.1 on average, 4 at most** | **18,421, 34,131, 65,437** | **14,818** |
| 2,000 by 3,000 | 1.3 to 1.8 on average, 4 at most | 63,418, 94,209, 171,127 | 4,245 |

With that reader the tile of 1,000 by 1,500 kept a walk to two shards on average and four at most, a median of 18 KB downtown and 4 KB to 10 KB elsewhere, against a file count a third of the smaller tile's. A walk now reads the 3 by 3 tiles around the tile of each end (below), 9 tiles when the ends share a tile and at most 14 for ends up to 400 m apart; a window is about 31 KB gzip at the median and 122 KB at the most in downtown Seattle, figures of the GOB-468 design review on 2026-11-14 over build 189b257fecb2d46b, the sum of the gzip sizes of the nine shards around a tile, which I have not re-measured.

Over the real shards, 600 walks between two stops of the stored responses, 60 to 400 m apart in a straight line, the stops read from the stored `stops-for-route` responses, found a path for all 600, at a median of 1.13 times the straight line, a 90th percentile of 1.52, and 1.0 ms on average to plan on a development machine. Walks between random points are not a measure of this: they end in water, fields, and blocks that no way enters, so their lengths count a connector to the nearest way. Before the snap chose a component both ends reach, 594 of 600 stop-to-stop walks were found.

## Publication

`Gobusgo.WalkGraph.Publication` orders and encodes each tile's pieces, stores each shard at `intersections/walk-graph-<build identifier>-<row>_<column>.bin.gz` and the index at `intersections/walk-graph-index-<SHA-256 of the stream>.bin.gz` through `Gobusgo.Writer` with a reader keep time of a year, then, only after every file is stored, the manifest at `intersections/walking-manifest-<UTC build time>-<SHA-256 of the index>.json`: canonical JSON of the shape `components.schemas.WalkGraphManifest` describes in `priv/openapi.json`. The writer stores a destination once, so each build's manifest has a name of its own, and the reader takes the newest by name with `Gobusgo.Reader.discover/2` (`Publication.manifest/1`). The manifests of earlier builds are deleted only after the new one is stored, and a file of the same name is left as it is.

The files go in the `intersections/` directory, under the prefixes `walk-graph-` and `walking-manifest-`, and not under a directory of their own (decided in GOB-467): the graph is a derivative database of OpenStreetMap under the same license, CloudFront already serves `/intersections/*` from the bucket and `GobusgoWeb.Endpoint` serves the directory locally, so production serves the graph with no change to the infrastructure repository, and the manifest prefix differs from the intersections' (`intersections/manifest-`) so neither build's discovery or cleanup sees the other's. The cost is a name that does not say walking; the kinds' prefixes do. Nothing deletes the data files: `Gobusgo.RetentionPolicy` has no rule for them, the hourly cleanup leaves them alone, and the bucket has no lifecycle rule for `intersections/walk-graph-`, so the files of an earlier build stay until they are removed by hand (an old build is 11 MB).

## Serving

`GET /api/walk-graph/manifest` answers the manifest through `Gobusgo.Reader`, whatever backend stores it, as the intersection manifest is answered (`lib/gobusgo_web/AGENTS.md`); its response is described by `components.schemas.WalkGraphManifest` in `priv/openapi.json`. `GobusgoWeb.Endpoint` serves the files whose name starts with `walk-graph-` from the intersections directory with a plug of their own, beside the one for those that start with `intersections-`, each keep time owned by its publication module (`Gobusgo.WalkGraph.Publication.reader_keep_seconds/0`), as the gzip streams they are stored as, `application/gzip` with no content encoding, `public, max-age=31536000, immutable`. Production serves `/intersections/*` from the bucket through CloudFront and `/api/walk-graph/manifest` from the application, so the files need nothing of the infrastructure repository.

## The task

`mix gobusgo.walk_graph.build --extract <path to .osm.pbf> [--bounds south,west,north,east] [--working-directory <dir>]` runs four `osmium` commands through `Gobusgo.Command`, each with a 10-minute deadline, selects, cuts, stores the files and the manifest, and prints the step times, the counts, and the sizes of the files. It never runs on a start. `fileinfo` reads the source date, `tags-filter` keeps the ways of the highway values, `extract -s complete_ways -b` cuts the bounds, and `cat -f opl,add_metadata=false` writes the ways and nodes as text. The selection reads the export three times and holds ETS tables of the nodes' positions and of the ways' references: on 2026-11-14 the whole task took 107 s and peaked at a maximum resident size of 1,587 MB (`/usr/bin/time -l`), against the intersections' 334 MB, which a hand-run build on a development machine has room for and the application's start never meets. osmium is `osmium-tool` 1.19.1, as for the places file and the intersections. The tests run a stand-in.

## In the browser

`assets/js/walk_path_reader.js` is held by the `TripPlanner` hook. For the chosen journey `journeyWalkEnds` names each walk's two ends, the stops or the rider's position or the destination point, in degrees, the position staying in the pure `planWalk` and never in a request: a fetch names a tile, and the walk's ends decide which. What the requests reveal to the file host is the union of the fixed 3 by 3 tiles around the tile of each end, which is a function of the two end tiles alone, and whether a fetch happens depends on those tiles alone, since the distance between the ends is checked once the tiles are held. The host learns each end's tile, about 1.1 km, wherever in it the end stands, and, where the two tiles differ, that the ends are near each other: the walks are a walk long, at most 400 m by the router's radius, so each end is known to within about a walk's length of the other's tile, not the whole tile. A stop end is public already, and the rider's position is the end that this places. The reader fetches the manifest once per page, `/api/walk-graph/manifest`, checks it with `acceptWalkManifest`, fetches the index it names, and then each shard a walk reads, one fetch per tile, `assets/js/walk_graph_fetch.js` the one fetcher, with a 20 s timeout each and the index checked against the size the manifest names. The decoded tiles are kept to 256 on a page, the 16 walks a request may name times the 16 tiles a walk may read (`HELD_TILE_COUNT_MAX`), so the tiles of one request never evict one another, and `request` asserts that its walks fit; a read of a walk's tiles marks them the most recently used, so the oldest tiles of earlier requests go first. A decoded shard holds 8 bytes a position, and the largest of the first build, about 40 KB expanded, decodes to about 80 KB: the cap grew fourfold from 64 tiles and holds at most about 20 MB, where 14 tiles of a downtown walk hold well under 1 MB. Each arrival plans the walks again, and a line found draws the journey again, as an arriving trip path does (`lib/gobusgo/routing/AGENTS.md`).

`planWalk` returns a kind-keyed value: `found` with the line's coordinates and its length, `noPath` with a reason (`tooFar` for ends more than 1,500 m apart, checked once the tiles are held, or two windows that together hold more than 16 tiles, whose walks keep the straight line, `noShards` for ends in tiles the index lacks, `offGraph` for an end more than 100 m from any piece, `disconnected` for ends that reach no component in common, and `capReached` for a search past 30,000 settled nodes or a graph past 250,000 pieces), or `notLoaded` with the tiles still to fetch. A walk with any answer but `found` keeps the straight line, and so does a walk whose tile fails to load, which the reader does not fetch again until the walks asked for change. A new request retries the manifest 60 s after it failed.

The tiles a walk reads are the 3 by 3 tiles around the tile of each end that the index holds; the pieces of those tiles are the graph, so a detour that leaves the windows is not found, and the walk takes the longer path through the pieces held or, with none, keeps the straight line. A walk whose windows hold more than 16 tiles is `tooFar` and keeps the straight line without a fetch, and one whose ends are more than 1,500 m apart keeps it after the tiles are fetched. The 16-tile cap is a backstop that the limits cannot reach for the walks the router makes: a walk of up to 400 m reads at most 14 tiles, and any path of up to about 2.2 km stays inside the two windows. A piece belongs only to the tile of its first position, so a piece whose segment crosses a tile that holds none of its positions is missing when only that tile loads, and the walk through it finds a longer path or none; this is rare, the format keeps it, and the straight line stays where no path is found. The path is A* over the pieces' junctions, the straight distance in a plane tangent at the walk's middle as its heuristic, with each end snapped as a virtual node that joins its piece's two ends, to the nearest point of a component that both ends reach within 100 m, the pair whose distances sum least (a component is a connected set of pieces, found by union-find over the nodes), so an island of footway nearer an end than the street does not strand the walk; the line runs from the end to its snap, along the path, and from the other snap to the other end.

The files are not in the offline bundle (decided in GOB-467): the service worker saves only `/tiles/`, `/routing/`, and the places file, a walk drawn offline keeps the straight line, and 11 MB of the whole region is more than a rider saves for the minutes a walk's line is worth. A page that has fetched a tile has it in the browser's cache, which serves it again by its immutable name.

## Search on the server (GOB-468)

`Gobusgo.WalkGraph.Search` and `Gobusgo.WalkGraph.Graph` are the Elixir side of `assets/js/core/walk_path.js`, so a snapshot's walking links and the walks a page plans in the browser are measured over one graph by one set of rules (decided in GOB-468). They take shards as `Gobusgo.WalkGraph.Format.decode_shard/1` returns them and read no file. The snapshot's walking links use them where `config :gobusgo, :walk` names the streets (`lib/gobusgo/routing/AGENTS.md`, "Walking links along the streets"): `Search.prepare/1` builds the graph once over the pieces within 750 m of a quarter of a tile on a plane tangent at its middle, and `Search.reach_prepared/1` answers each stop of the tile from it, so a stop's distances are on that plane and differ from the drawn walk's, whose plane is tangent at the middle of its two ends, by at most 1.3 cm over 392 links of the real table (a median of 0.6 mm, a 90th percentile of 2.4 mm, measured on 2026-11-14). The browser's position walks are a later change of GOB-468.

- **`Search.tiles_needed/1`** is `walkTilesNeeded`: the 3 by 3 tiles around the tile of each end that the index holds, in row then column order, or `:too_far` where the two windows hold more than `tile_count_max` tiles. `Search.plan/1` is `planWalk`: the tiles the held map lacks as `{:not_loaded, tiles}`, then the distance check of `distance_meters_max` once they are held, then `find_path/1`.
- **`Search.find_path/1`** is `findWalkPath`: a plane tangent at the middle of the two ends, an A* search between the snapped ends bounded by `visited_node_count_max` settled nodes and `edge_count_max` pieces, and `{:found, meters}` or `{:no_path, reason}` with the reasons of `NoPathReason`.
- **`Search.reach/1`** is one start and up to `target_count_max` targets in one multi-source Dijkstra over a plane tangent at the start, settled in order of cost up to `street_meters_max` (600 m, less each component's start snap) and `visited_node_count_max` nodes. A target connected to the start by street within the bound is `{:found, meters}`; a connected one past it is `{:no_path, :beyond_bound}`. A search that hits the node bound stopped at a frontier cost: a target whose street is no longer than the frontier is still exact, since every node cheaper than the frontier is settled, and every other target that shares a component with the start is `:cap_reached`.
- **The snap rules are the browser's.** A point snaps to the nearest point of each component within `snap_meters_max`; for two points the shared component with the least summed snap distance wins. Distances within a micrometer tie, and a tie goes to the lesser start distance, then the lesser x and y, then the lesser ends of the edge, then the lesser start of the segment, all geometry, so the order of the shards, the component numbers, and the node numbers change no answer. `snapsByComponent` and `chooseSnaps` of the browser carry the same keys. A `Graph` holds its segments in a grid of 100 m cells, so a snap reads the cells within the snap bound and not every segment.
- **A golden file holds the two sides together.** `test/fixtures/walk_links/v1/expected.json` is the browser's answer to 168 pairs of points over the shards of `test/fixtures/walk_graph/v1/` and two shards of its own, which are in the file, in the index's empty tiles (-3, -5) and (-3, -4): two parallel streets that tie for a pair of ends, the northern with a bump so the street a tie chooses changes the length, and a long street across the two tiles. Each pair holds the tiles it reads. `node test/scripts/walk_links_fixtures.mjs` writes it and `node test/scripts/walk_links_check.mjs` recomputes it in the core. `test/gobusgo/walk_graph/search_test.exs` reads it and agrees within 0.1 m through its own tile selection, for `plan/1` and, one target at a time, for `reach/1` over the tiles of both ends and over the tiles around the start alone. The planes differ (the middle of a pair against the start), which is the whole of the gap. A test also holds `Search.limits/0` to the browser's limits in the file.
- **A walk from `reach/1` reads the start's window alone.** A link or a position walk is planned over the 3 by 3 tiles around the start, where the drawn walk reads the windows of both ends, so a component that joins only through a loop outside the start's window is disconnected for the plan and joined for the drawing. That is rare, and accepted (decided in GOB-468); a test pins it. It is the same kind of limit as the tile ownership of a piece above: both leave a path out of the graph a search holds.
- **Parity on real shards was measured once and is not a CI check.** On 2026-11-14, over 1,500 walks between stops of the real graph, the two sides selected the same tiles and agreed to 5e-11 m, and `reach/1` agreed with the browser on all of them; the script is not kept in the repository.
- **Cost.** Measured on 2026-11-14 on a development machine over a synthetic window of 35,378 pieces: building the graph 160 to 190 ms and 71 MB, one snap under 1 ms, 256 snaps 80 ms, `reach/1` with 64 targets 0.2 s and with 256 targets 0.3 s, almost all of it the build. A `plan/1` over the real shards of its 3 by 3 windows, 9 to 14 tiles, about 35 ms, most of it building the graph, which `reach/1` does once for all its targets. The cost of a whole snapshot's links is not measured.

## Checked in a browser (GOB-467)

On 2026-11-14 in headless Chrome against a local server over the files of the build above, the page's `liveSocket` gave the running `TripPlanner` hook, which was given a journey of one walk from a rider position, and the hook's `draw` ran the reader, the pure plan, and the map's layer as a page does. The routing manifest of the local data directory held no snapshot, so no journey was planned through the Trip tab itself; the walk's two ends were set by hand. A walk from Pike Place Market to a point 400 m north-east fetched the manifest, the index, and the four shards its box touches, found a path of 46 positions, and drew it along the streets, through Victor Steinbrueck Park's paths and up First Avenue, where the straight line crossed blocks. A walk of 352 m across Interstate 5 found a path of 499 m with 43 positions. With the network offline after the page loaded, the manifest failed to load, no line was held, and the walk stayed the straight line.

## Open

- The planned time of a walk stays the router's straight-line time; GOB-468 plans over this graph, which carries the class and a position's neighbors for it.
- A road with a mapped sidewalk and no `sidewalk` tag on it is walked on the centerline where it is the shorter, which the drawing shows as the street's line and not the sidewalk's.
- Whether the graph is rebuilt on a schedule: it is built by hand, as the intersections are.
- The build's peak memory, 1.6 GB, is accepted for a task run by hand; a leaner selection pass is open.
